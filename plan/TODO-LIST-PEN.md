### The work

- **`task-soup`** — chemicals as opaque IDs carrying concentrations and half-lives. Meaning
  lives in the relations wired around a chemical, never parsed out of its name. Scher's
  opaque-slug discipline wearing a lab coat: same law, same reason.
- **`task-genome`** — reactions, receptors, emitters, and lobe shape all expressed from a
  genome, even while v0 ships exactly one hand-written genome and no breeding. Breeding then
  falls out nearly free instead of demanding a rewrite.
- **`task-lobe`** — one small Hebbian lobe floating in the soup. A brain with no body
  produces arbitrary behavior; the soup gives the net something to mean.
- **`task-viewer`** — the brain-and-chemistry instrument, day one. In every real Creatures
  community the fun lived in *watching*, and building instruments for watching processes
  describes this workshop's whole practice anyway.
- **`task-muslin-tick-ceiling`** — **done**, `muslin/tick-ceiling.test.ts`. Findings below.
- **`task-port-streams`** — named seeded streams per port (mutation, diffusion, tie-break,
  spawn), with interventions logged as exogenous events at named ports. Addressable ports
  buy counterfactuals as an instrument: replay a life with every stream fixed but one, and
  "bad genes or bad weather?" turns into an experiment rather than a vibe. A seeded
  `mulberry32` already sits in the muslin as the toy-scale ancestor of this.
- **`task-gist-raster-window`** — full actuality for the recent window, gists behind it,
  rasters behind that: a mipmap of the past. Seeded determinism pays for honest perishing —
  raster plus seed must regenerate any window by re-simulation.
- **`task-birth-prehends-parents`** — the lineage edges themselves.

## Findings — `wonder-tick-ceiling`, answered 2026-08-14

Measured by `muslin/tick-ceiling.test.ts` at 200 creatures × 6 chemicals, against a 4 Hz
tick (250 ms per tick, of which the soup may spend 25 ms):

| substrate | ms/tick |
|---|---|
| raw `Float64Array` (no scher) | ~0.004 |
| one `Cell` per creature, whole vector | ~0.008 |
| one `Cell` per chemical | ~0.04–0.09 |
| one appended beat per chemical | ~1.6–1.8 |

1. **Every substrate fits, including the append-only one.** The session's prediction held
   that appending a beat per chemical per tick would die of its own honesty. On compute it
   does not — it lands ~14× under the soup's allowance.
2. **`lay()` holds its cost flat as the log grows.** Appending the 400,000th row costs what
   appending the first cost; the Map and adjacency indexes hold.
3. **Room runs out, not time.** Every row stays retained by construction. That single fact
   relocates the whole architecture question.
4. **Therefore `wish-perishing-holds` carries weight rather than polish.** Perishing is what
   makes chemistry-in-scher viable at all, and it needs no retention policy — the grammar
   already supplies one. Still-being-prehended IS the retention rule.

The correction matters more than the confirmation: the mechanism guessed in session (an
append-per-tick substrate cannot hold) turned out right about *what* breaks and wrong about
*why*. Anyone reading the earlier reasoning should take the numbers over the prose.
